SECTION B — NUTRITION & PERFORMANCE REFERENCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
GOAL: Cut to competition weight while maintaining daily MMA
performance and cognitive sharpness for 2+ hours of deep work.
Core principles and the medical guardrail live in the system
instruction. This is the detailed reference.

STANDING DAILY HABITS (keep in every plan unless he says otherwise):
  Beef bone broth — PRE-WORKOUT (~6:30 AM). Collagen/glycine, joint and
    gut support, electrolytes, light warm fuel that won't sit heavy
    before MMA. Take it in place of, or alongside, the banana/dates.
  Haldi wala doodh (turmeric milk) + a pinch of black pepper — before
    sleep. The black pepper (piperine) sharply boosts curcumin
    absorption; anti-inflammatory for daily-MMA recovery and a calming
    wind-down. Fits the pre-10:15 PM sleep routine.

PREFERRED PROTEINS (use these, in any combination):
  Rawas, surmai, or salmon — omega-3 priority, 3–4x/week
  Chicken (breast or thigh) — most versatile, cook in bulk
  Lean beef (mince/keema or small cuts) — iron, zinc, creatine, B12
  Prawns — fast, lean, high zinc
  Eggs — always available, works in any meal; his standing protein floor
  Paneer — vegetarian protein; pan-sear or add to curries; great for a
    no-meat day

  ORGAN MEATS (weekly rotation — nutrient density a cut needs; FREQUENCY-
  CAPPED → read [[ORGAN MEAT — FREQUENCY & SAFETY]] below before cooking):
  Liver — kaleji (chicken) or beef liver. Nature's whole-food multivitamin:
    B12, folate, copper, heme iron, vitamin A, choline. CAPPED — never daily.
  Kidney — gurda. B12, selenium, riboflavin, CoQ10 — without liver's vit-A load.
  Brain — bheja. DHA omega-3 + choline for CNS / impact recovery; rich, occasional.
  Marrow — nalli. Fat, collagen, glycine — joints and connective tissue.

DAIRY (protein + fats — keep in the fridge):
  Milk — protein + calcium; in haldi doodh, or a glass with a meal
  Curd / dahi — gut-friendly probiotic, North Indian staple; cooling,
    pairs with any meal (a bowl after lunch)
  Cheese — protein + fat, but calorie-dense → measured amounts on the cut

CARB SOURCES (in order of preference):
  Rice (white or brown) — primary carb, North Indian staple
  Roti / chapati — flexible, goes with any curry
  Dal (lentils) — counts as both carb AND protein supplement
  Fresh seasonal fruits — vitamins + fibre; favour them around training
    (post-MMA), keep portions modest on the cut and lighter in the evening.
    VALUE RULE: buy in-season + locally grown — that is the cheapest, freshest,
    most nutritious fruit. Out-of-season / imported = cold-stored, pricey, less
    fresh → skip it. Heuristics to state: "what's piled high and cheap at the
    mandi this week is in peak season"; "if it feels pricey for the season, it's
    imported — pick the local champion instead." DON'T read a fixed calendar —
    the Gem reasons from TODAY's date + the user's location (established per the
    system instruction, not hardcoded) to name the current best-value local pick:
    the region's own peak-season produce, including its prized local / GI
    specialties when they're in season (harvest windows shift ±1–2 weeks year to
    year). Whatever is locally abundant and cheap that week is in season; bananas
    and similar staples are cheap local year-round defaults

VEGETABLES (necessary only, easy to include):
  Spinach (saag) — wilts invisible into any curry in 2 mins
  Onion, tomato, garlic, ginger — base of every North Indian dish
    Not a sacrifice — they ARE the cooking
  Rajma (kidney beans) — high protein, North Indian classic
  Chana (chickpeas) — protein + fibre, easy to batch

HEALTHY FATS & COOKING OILS (calorie-dense — measure on the cut):
  Ghee — cooking/finishing in small spoonfuls; a clean North Indian fat
  Mustard oil — traditional North Indian cooking oil; pungent, heart-friendly
  Olive oil — extra-virgin, for finishing / salads (not high-heat)
  Avocado — whole-food fat + fibre; ¼–½ at a meal, pairs well with eggs
  Beef tallow — rendered beef fat; smoke point ~204°C, oxidation-stable, ideal
    for high-heat searing / bhuna and frying eggs. ~50% saturated → fine DAILY
    as a cooking fat (~1–2 tbsp) WITHIN the saturated-fat budget. Render it from
    suet yourself (see repertoire); keeps months. Always a clean, dry spoon.
  Bone marrow (nalli) — fat + collagen + glycine; treat as a calorie/fat source
    (~770 kcal/100g) and count it toward the day's fat. 1–2x/week.
  Note: fats fuel hormones and satiety, but 1g fat = 9 kcal — keep total
  fat measured so the 300–400 kcal deficit holds, and keep bigger fats away
  from the pre-MMA window so they don't sit heavy.

ORGAN MEAT — FREQUENCY & SAFETY (read before cooking any organ meat):
RULE OF THUMB: "Liver is medicine, not a staple." Organ meats are nutrient-
dense — that density is exactly why they are capped. Tallow is the only daily
item; bone broth (a standing habit) is also daily.

  WEEKLY PATTERN (one person):
  Liver (beef OR chicken) ... 1 serving/week — vitamin A is the limiter
  Kidney (gurda) ........... 1–2x/week
  Brain (bheja) ............ at most 1x/week, small portion
  Marrow (nalli) ........... 1–2x/week (calorie-dense — mind total fat)
  Tallow ................... daily cooking fat, ~1–2 tbsp
  Bone broth ............... daily (already his habit)

  GUARDRAILS (CRITICAL — honour these):
  - LIVER / vitamin A: the upper limit for preformed vitamin A (retinol) is
    ~3,000 µg/day. Beef liver holds ~9,000 µg per 100g — a single 100g serving
    is roughly 3× the daily UL, so it is fine OCCASIONALLY but NEVER daily.
    Beef liver: 100–150g, 1x/week. Chicken liver is milder (~3,300 µg/100g):
    100–150g, 1–2x/week. Don't eat both in the same week beyond one serving,
    and don't stack a vitamin-A / cod-liver-oil supplement on top.
  - BRAIN / prions + cholesterol: cooking does NOT destroy prions, and brain is
    extremely high in cholesterol. Keep it occasional (≤1x/week, small), and
    source from YOUNG, healthy animals. Goat/lamb bheja from young animals is a
    common lower-concern choice in India.
  - KIDNEY / purines: high-purine. Fine 1–2x/week for a healthy, hydrated
    athlete; AVOID / limit with gout, high uric acid, or kidney-stone history.
  - MARROW / tallow: calorie- and saturated-fat-dense. Keep within the daily
    fat budget (saturated fat <10% of calories); "stable fat" is not "unlimited."
  Note: organ meats already supply B12, heme iron, and zinc — so do NOT also
  take a B12/iron/zinc supplement on top (see SUPPLEMENTS + the system
  instruction's MEDICAL GUARDRAIL).

ORGAN MEAT — SOURCING (India):
  Buy from a halal / qassab (butcher) shop — kaleji, gurda, bheja, and nalli
  bones are standard offal there. ORDER brain and marrow ahead; ask for the
  same-day-slaughter batch (and for suet / kidney fat for tallow — often cheap
  or free). Freshness signs: liver glossy and firm (no grey, no sour smell);
  kidney firm with a faint clean smell (strong ammonia = old); brain pale
  pink-grey, moist, intact; marrow pink/cream, filling the bone. Refrigerate
  within 1–2 hours; raw organ meat keeps only 1–2 days in the fridge — cook or
  freeze it fast, in single-serving portions; thaw in the fridge, never on the counter.

FALLBACK MEALS (solo — when prep didn't happen, or he's eating out):
  He lives alone, so there is no default "someone cooked." When the batch ran
  out or a cook got skipped, pick one — no guilt, no macro-policing:
  - Eggs + curd (dahi) + fruit — boiled eggs are already in the fridge; zero cook.
  - Bone broth + microwave rice pouch + an egg cracked in — ~4 minutes.
  - Eat out / order in — gauge protein; if it's mostly carbs (dal-rice, biryani,
    sabzi-roti), add 2–3 boiled eggs after. Consistency over perfection.
  Keep the fallback staples ALWAYS stocked (eggs, curd, fruit, broth in the
  freezer, a rice pouch) — they're the safety net that makes the system stick.

MEAL PREP — BATCH MODEL & FOOD-SAFETY STORAGE:
The system is "cook once, eat 3–4 times." (Principles live in the MEAL PREP
PROTOCOL in the system instruction; this is the detail.)

  THE BATCH (one weekly cook session, ~60–90 min, mostly passive):
  - Anchor protein → buy ~1.5–2 kg raw, cook to ~1–1.5 kg. Portion AT COOK TIME
    into ~150–200 g servings; 3–4 to the fridge, the rest frozen the same day.
  - Dal → a big pot; portion, freeze half.
  - Boiled eggs → a dozen at week start, kept IN-SHELL.
  - Bone broth → batch the bones; keep a few days' worth in the fridge, freeze
    the rest in cups + ice-cube trays (instant flavour base for curries/dal).
  - Organ meat → a SEPARATE small batch (1–2 portions only — frequency-capped).
  MIDWEEK 15-MIN TOP-UP: a fresh small batch of rice (shortest safe life), a few
  more eggs, and pull 2–3 protein portions freezer → fridge.

  STORAGE TABLE (fridge ≤4°C / freezer −18°C; freezer times = quality, not safety):
  Food                          Fridge      Freezer
  Cooked chicken (curry/pieces)  3–4 days    4–6 months
  Cooked beef / mutton           3–4 days    2–3 months
  Cooked keema (ground)          3–4 days    2–3 months   (ground spoils faster)
  Cooked fish / prawns           3–4 days    2–3 months   (prawns lean to 2–3 d)
  Cooked RICE                    1–2 days    1–2 months   ← THE RISK ITEM
  Dal / cooked lentils           3–5 days    ~6 months    (3 d if creamy/ghee-heavy)
  Boiled eggs (in shell)         ~1 week     don't freeze
  Marinated RAW meat             1–2 days    3–6 months   (freeze raw-in-marinade)
  Bone broth / stock             3–4 days    4–6 months
  Organ meat (cooked, offal)     1–2 days    1–2 months   (perishable; keep batch small)
  Nihari / marrow-shank stew     2–3 days    2–3 months   (gravy stew keeps better)

  RICE / Bacillus cereus (the one thing that can genuinely make him sick):
  spores survive reheating — cool rice FAST, fridge within ~1 hour, eat within
  1–2 days, REHEAT ONCE only, to steaming. This is why rice is the midweek
  top-up, not the prep-day bulk cook.

  UNIVERSAL RULES (USDA / NHS):
  - 2-hour rule: cooked food into the fridge within 2 h (1 h if the kitchen is hot).
  - Don't leave food out to cool fully — get it down fast in shallow containers.
  - Reheat to 74°C / 165°F, steaming through. Reheat a portion ONCE.
  - Don't refreeze cooked food that was thawed + reheated.
  - Thaw in the fridge (or sealed bag under cold water), never on the counter.
  - When in doubt, throw it out.

SUPPLEMENTS — GRADED (detailed reference; see MEDICAL GUARDRAIL in the system
instruction — the user always clears anything new with his doctor). Supplements
are the last ~5%; the foundation (protein, sleep, hydration/sodium, fuelling
the session) comes first.

  TIER 1 — strong evidence, high value, do these:
  - Creatine monohydrate — 5g daily, any time (no loading needed). Best evidence
    for repeated high-intensity effort (MMA's energy system) AND cognition;
    helps retain muscle in a deficit.
  - Hydration + electrolytes — fluids to pale-yellow urine; around the daily
    sweaty session add sodium ~300–700 mg per hour of sweat (more in heat), plus
    potassium + magnesium from food/mix. Don't under-replace sodium.
  - Omega-3 — fatty fish (rawas/surmai/salmon) 3–4x/week is the gold standard;
    on non-fish days, fish oil ~1–2 g combined EPA+DHA. Don't stack on fish days.
  - Sleep (the highest-ROI "supplement") — protect 7–9 h, consistent schedule.

  TIER 2 — moderate / emerging / situational:
  - Magnesium glycinate — 200–350 mg elemental, 30–60 min pre-sleep (pairs with
    the haldi doodh wind-down). Stay at/under the 350 mg/day SUPPLEMENTAL upper
    limit unless the physician directs higher. Aids sleep; may ease cramps/DOMS.
  - Collagen + vitamin C — 15 g collagen + ~50 mg vit C, 30–60 min BEFORE
    training (training days only). Supports tendon/ligament resilience under
    MMA's joint load; the pre-load timing is what matters.
  - Caffeine — 3–6 mg/kg ~60 min pre-session. He trains ~7 AM, so sleep risk is
    minimal. More is not better.
  - Whey protein isolate — 1–2 scoops as needed to hit 2.0–2.2 g/kg. Pure
    convenience lever for a solo athlete in a deficit; adherence is what counts.
  - Beetroot/nitrate (6–8 mmol, 2–3 h pre) and tart cherry (around hard blocks /
    competition) — optional fine-tuning; smaller effects in trained athletes.

  ANYTHING A DOCTOR PRESCRIBES — take as directed; don't assume or invent a
  prescription he hasn't named (see MEDICAL GUARDRAIL).
  Red meat + weekly organ meats already cover B12 / iron / zinc — don't double-dose.

CUT / TRAINING — WHAT NOT TO OVER-RESTRICT:
  - Sodium: don't strip it. Daily sweaty training + a deficit is exactly when
    low sodium causes flat sessions, cramps, and lightheadedness. Only
    manipulate water/sodium deliberately in the FINAL days of a true weigh-in
    cut — then aggressively rehydrate + replace sodium after weigh-in.
    Physician-coordinated; never train hard dehydrated + sodium-stripped.
  - Carbs around training: keep them pre and/or post-session even in a deficit —
    they protect high-intensity output. Take the deficit out of the 9 sedentary
    desk hours, not the session's fuel.
  - Fat: don't go very-low-fat — it impairs absorption of fat-soluble vitamins
    (A/D/E/K) and hormonal health. Keep adequate dietary fat.
  - Antioxidant timing: don't chronically megadose antioxidants (large tart-
    cherry / vitamin-C loads) right around every session — it can blunt training
    adaptation. Use tart cherry tactically around competition / heavy blocks.

STARTER RECIPE REPERTOIRE (North Indian, batch / solo)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Compact cards the Gem can serve or adapt — or generate its own (Directive 2).
Storage numbers match the storage table above; serve via the RECIPE FORMAT
CONTRACT. GENERAL ORGAN-MEAT PREP: soak liver/kidney/brain in salted or turmeric
water 20–30 min and rinse (cuts odour/blood); cook organ meats JUST to done —
overcooking makes them rubbery. Use beef tallow (card F) or ghee/mustard oil.

A. CHICKEN KEEMA (the go-to anchor batch — non-organ)
  Yield 4–5 portions · fridge 3–4 d · freeze 2–3 mo.
  Ingredients: 1 kg chicken mince; 2 onions; 2 tomatoes; 1.5 tbsp ginger-garlic;
    2 green chillies; 1 tsp cumin; 1 tsp turmeric; 1.5 tsp red chilli; 1.5 tsp
    coriander powder; 1 tsp garam masala; 1 cup peas (optional); 3 tbsp tallow/
    ghee; salt; coriander.
  Steps: 1) Fry cumin + onions golden in fat. 2) Ginger-garlic, then tomatoes +
    dry spices to a thick masala. 3) Add mince, brown, breaking lumps. 4) Splash
    water, cover, simmer ~15 min till cooked. 5) Garam masala + coriander.
  Time: 10 prep + 25 cook · Protein ~30 g/portion.
  Reheat: pan/microwave with a splash of water. Cut tweak: lean mince, less fat,
    pair with salad instead of rice.

B. KALEJI MASALA (chicken or beef liver) — ORGAN, cap 1x/week (vitamin A)
  Yield 4 portions · fridge 1–2 d · freeze 1 mo.
  Ingredients: 500 g liver (cubed, sinew trimmed); 2 onions; 2 tomatoes; 1.5 tbsp
    ginger-garlic; 2 green chillies; 1 tsp cumin; 1 tsp turmeric; 1.5 tsp red
    chilli; 1.5 tsp coriander powder; 1 tsp garam masala; 3 tbsp tallow/ghee; salt.
  Steps: 1) Soak liver in turmeric water 20 min, drain. 2) Fry cumin + onions
    golden. 3) Ginger-garlic, tomatoes + dry spices to a thick masala. 4) Add
    liver, sear high 5–7 min JUST until no longer pink — don't overcook.
    5) Garam masala + coriander.
  Time: 15 prep + 20 cook · Protein ~28–33 g/portion.
  Reheat: gentle, with a splash of water — don't boil hard.
  FREQUENCY: the weekly liver slot. With beef liver, eat ONE serving and freeze
    the rest — don't eat all four in one week (vitamin-A cap).

C. BHEJA FRY (brain) — ORGAN, cap ≤1x/week, small; young-animal source
  Yield 3–4 portions · fridge 1–2 d · best fresh (freezing hurts texture).
  Ingredients: 400 g brain; 1 onion (fine); 1 tomato; 1 tbsp ginger-garlic;
    2 green chillies; 1 tsp turmeric; 1 tsp red chilli; 1 tsp coriander powder;
    ½ tsp garam masala; 2 tbsp tallow/ghee; salt; coriander/mint.
  Steps: 1) Soak in salted water 30 min, peel the thin membrane, rinse.
    2) Optional parboil 3–4 min, break into chunks. 3) Fry onion soft; add
    ginger-garlic, tomato + spices to a paste. 4) Fold in brain, cook 8–10 min
    low-medium till set (scrambles like soft eggs). 5) Garam masala + herbs.
  Time: 35 (incl. soak) + 15 cook · Protein ~12–18 g/portion (fat-rich, lower protein).
  Reheat: low heat only.

D. GURDA BHUNA (kidney) — ORGAN, 1–2x/week; skip if gout/uric-acid
  Yield 4 portions · fridge 1–2 d · freeze 1 mo.
  Ingredients: 500 g kidney (split, white core removed, diced); 2 onions;
    2 tomatoes; 1.5 tbsp ginger-garlic; 1 tsp cumin; 1 tsp turmeric; 1.5 tsp red
    chilli; 1.5 tsp coriander powder; 1 tsp garam masala; 3 tbsp tallow; salt.
  Steps: 1) Soak diced kidney in salted/turmeric water 30 min, rinse twice
    (cuts ammonia). 2) Optional 3-min parboil, drain. 3) Bhuna base: cumin +
    onions golden, ginger-garlic, tomatoes + spices to an oil-separating masala.
    4) Add kidney, bhuna high 8–10 min till just cooked. 5) Garam masala + coriander.
  Time: 35 (incl. soak) + 20 cook · Protein ~24–28 g/portion.
  Reheat: quick pan with a splash of water. Drink water (purines).

E. SIMPLIFIED NALLI NIHARI / MARROW — 1–2x/week (rich; mind total fat)
  Yield 4 portions · fridge 2–3 d · freeze 2–3 mo (freezes well).
  Ingredients: 4–6 marrow bones (~1–1.2 kg) OR 800 g bone-in shank + 2 marrow
    bones; 2 onions; 2 tbsp ginger-garlic; 3 tbsp tallow/ghee; 2 tbsp nihari
    masala; 1 tsp turmeric; 3 tbsp atta (wholewheat) to thicken; salt; ginger
    juliennes, lemon, coriander to serve.
  Steps: 1) Brown onions in fat, add ginger-garlic, then dry spices + a splash
    of water. 2) Add bones, sear, cover with water. 3) Slow-simmer 2.5–4 h (or
    pressure-cook ~45 min) till marrow soft + meat falls off; push the marrow
    into the gravy. 4) Atta + water slurry, stir in, simmer 10 min. 5) Serve
    with ginger, lemon, coriander.
  Time: 20 prep + 45 min–4 h · Protein ~20–30 g/portion (with shank meat).
  Reheat: stovetop, loosen with water/broth.

F. RENDER BEEF TALLOW (cooking fat — make once, use for months)
  Yield ~700–800 g from 1 kg fat · pantry months / fridge ~1 yr / freezer ~indefinite.
  Ingredients: 1 kg beef fat / suet (kidney fat = cleanest); ¼ cup water; pinch salt.
  Steps: 1) Cut fat to ~1 cm (or have the butcher grind it). 2) Low heat with the
    splash of water. 3) Melt slowly 1.5–3 h, stirring; solids turn golden + sink —
    don't brown. 4) Strain through a fine sieve/muslin into clean DRY glass jars.
    5) Cool — it sets white/solid.
  Use: ~1 tbsp for bhuna, searing, frying eggs/parathas, roasting. Always a
    clean dry spoon (no water/crumbs) so it keeps.
