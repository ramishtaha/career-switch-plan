# V4 System Audit — 14 September 2026

## VERDICT

**Do not launch V4 as written.** It is another perfect-day plan, not a relapse-resistant system. Ramish has already shown that he can follow a precise plan for three days; the failure begins when phone use delays sleep and continues through re-sleeping, the post-camp phone spiral, office binging, and an unstructured evening. V4 schedules more actions inside that same fragile chain. A 26-year-old with ADHD, five weekday Muay Thai sessions, a TCS workday, solo food management, family obligations, and roughly ₹12 lakh debt does not need a more ambitious timetable. He needs fewer decision points and a system that changes automatically at the **first** break in the chain.

### Top five issues, ranked by impact

1. **The sleep assumption makes relapse likely, not exceptional.** `22:00 → 04:45` is only 6h45 in bed. It is even less actual sleep, and Ramish's own evidence is post-Fajr re-sleeping and post-camp napping. Optional tahajjud, five 90-minute camps, Saturday sparring, and possible extra sessions cannot sit on that sleep floor. The schedule is not resilient to a 30-minute slip: a 22:30 sleep gives 6h15, which recreates the known cascade.

2. **The post-camp danger zone is still being treated as a productivity window.** The only reliable chain was Fajr → gym; everything after gym broke. V4 puts a phone handoff, shake, shower, breakfast, new DSA, and a house reset between 08:45 and a fixed 10:30 departure. The timings also overlap: a 09:30–10:30 DSA block cannot coexist with a 10:15 house reset. One delayed shower or difficult problem makes him “late,” and ADHD all-or-nothing thinking can turn that into a discarded day.

3. **The day carries too many top-priority outputs.** Five obligatory prayers, camp, two DSA contacts, TCS, an optional second fitness session, a one-hour Spring block, a reel, dinner, check-in, family call, journal, and setup do not fit with recovery and transition time. The 19:15 Spring block begins six minutes after Maghrib, then runs into the 20:15 check-in, 20:26 Isha, and 20:30 family call. This is overpacked even before MMA fatigue or a late office departure.

4. **The relapse vector remains available in every weak period.** Removing the Instagram app is necessary but not sufficient. The main account carries Ramish's personal feed, messages, and social cues; laptop browser access can still become a binge. “Use office free time well” also relies on choice while the phone is physically present. Greyscale and intentions are weak controls against the exact behavior that produced nine consecutive misses.

5. **Restart and tracking mechanics reward novelty instead of recovery.** V1, V2, V2-restart, V3, and V4 in seven weeks have made planning a substitute for resuming. Completion-locked Day N, an 84-day grid, habit tracker, daily ledger, Rafiq streaks, and multiple specialist trackers create too many surfaces. Auto-pause correctly detected failure, but `paused` removes the next action when Ramish most needs one. V4 currently differs from V3 mainly by having more detailed timing.

## FIXES

### Minimal operating plan

**1. Protect sleep before adding optional worship or productivity.** For September prayer times, wake at about 04:55 for Fajr rather than 04:45 for tahajjud. Keep tahajjud optional and off the weekday schedule until sleep and the five obligatory prayers are stable; a nafl item must not undermine Fajr, health, or daytime functioning. Move the family call to immediately after Isha, approximately 20:35–20:55. Put the phone away at 20:55, do only a 10-minute setup, lights out at 21:15, and aim to be asleep by 21:30. That gives about 7h25 and leaves a 30-minute slip with nearly seven hours rather than 6h15.

All five salah remain the daily religious obligation. The existing “rebuild rungs” can be used as behavioral rescue prompts, but the system should not label a plan that intentionally omits Asr or Maghrib as compliant. Record misses plainly, resume at the next salah, and do not let one miss cancel the rest of the day. If Ramish wants a religious ruling on how to frame recovery, he should ask a trusted Hanafi scholar rather than let a productivity tracker define it.

**2. Turn 08:45–10:30 into an airlock, not a study block.** On arriving home: put the phone directly in the lockbox, drink the pre-portioned shake, and do not sit on the bed or sofa. Then shower, dress for office, eat the planned breakfast, take the packed bag, and leave at 10:30. Remove required post-camp DSA and the timed house reset. A 10-minute reset is optional only if he is fully ready by 10:15. After Fajr, pack/fuel and stay out of the bedroom; paper revision for 10–15 minutes is optional, but do not insert a required laptop session into the one Fajr → camp chain that already works.

**3. Give career work fewer, safer anchors.** Use one 25-minute DSA block during the office break, Monday–Friday, around 13:05 after Dhuhr and food. Use only an approved device/material under TCS policy. Schedule Spring Boot for 30 minutes on two weekday evenings plus one 45-minute Saturday block—not 60 minutes every night. Remove optional extra MMA sessions for the first two stable weeks; five camps plus Saturday sparring is already six training contacts. Monday/Thursday fasting evenings should not carry a heroic catch-up block after Maghrib.

A **minimum career attendance** is 10 minutes: open one named problem or one named Spring task and produce one note, test, or commit-sized change. It preserves continuity but does not advance a completion-locked “Day N.” Normal work can exceed it; missed work is never repaid the next day.

**4. Make phone control physical and location-specific.** At home, the phone goes into the lockbox on post-camp arrival and again immediately after the family call. At TCS, it goes zipped in the work bag or a locker from 12:30 until the 25-minute career block is marked; it does not sit face-down on the desk. Blockers and greyscale remain secondary layers, not the main control. Keep a small printed card in the bag containing only: `Dhuhr → food → phone away → 25-minute named task → one-line mark → walk/rest`.

**5. Keep documentary capture; isolate publishing.** Continue max-10-second transition clips and film-and-drop. Keep Instagram blocked on the phone. On the laptop, use a dedicated browser profile with no saved feed tabs, notifications, or personal browsing; block `/reels`, Explore, and the home feed where tooling permits. Open only the upload/scheduling surface, run a 10-minute timer, publish or schedule, then log out. Prepare the caption before opening Instagram. Laptop posting alone is **not** sufficient. If the main-account browser causes two browsing episodes in any seven days, the pre-decided response is to stop direct access and schedule posts through a publishing surface—or move publishing back to the separate account. Reach does not outrank the sleep and salah chain.

**6. Replace weekly meal-prep ambition with a three-portion protocol.** Ramish lives alone and Sunday batch cooking has failed, so “prep for the week” is too large. Use a fixed recurring grocery list—Ultra 7% milk, whey already owned, eggs, dates, frozen basa, and the ingredients already in the locked diet—within a defined monthly food cap appropriate to his debt. On Friday, reorder the same list. On Sunday, cook **one protein for three portions**, hard stop at 30 minutes; leave the remaining fish frozen. Each night, put whey powder in the shaker, place dates by the gym bag, and verify milk/eggs are present. Keep one diet-compliant emergency option available. Do not add recipe variety, seven containers, or another meal tracker. Nutritional adequacy and the fasting/training combination should be checked separately with a qualified clinician or dietitian if symptoms or recovery problems occur.

**7. Reduce tracking to one daily write and specialist evidence.** Keep `session-state.md` as the sole state source and `DAILY-LEDGER.md` as the only daily input. Keep the DSA tracker for problem evidence and the application tracker when applications actually begin. Archive or make read-only the duplicate 84-day grid and bullet habit tracker. Rafiq should display the source data; it should not require a second manual truth. A current streak may honestly be zero, but do not erase prior totals or create a fictional fresh history on 15 September.

### Explicit verdicts on the nine open questions

1. **Sleep math — Verdict: not sound and not slip-resilient.** 6h45 in bed is contradicted by Ramish's re-sleeping and post-camp naps. A 30-minute delay recreates the cascade. Remove weekday tahajjud for now, target roughly 21:30–04:55 sleep, and make the post-camp period phone-free with no required study.

2. **Career/MMA/family collision — Verdict: overpacked.** Daily 19:15–20:15 Spring Boot is unrealistic after a 90-minute camp and TCS, and it collides with Maghrib, Isha, and the family call. Put DSA in one 25-minute office anchor; reduce Spring Boot to two 30-minute weekday blocks plus one 45-minute Saturday block; keep the daily family call after Isha.

3. **Weekend structure — Verdict: the current blocks are too large and poorly anchored.** Saturday's 09:30–11:30 DSA leaves almost no transition/travel margin for 12:00 sparring in Mulund. Use: Fajr and phone parked; 09:15–09:45 DSA; leave with a real travel buffer; sparring; recovery meal; optional 45-minute Spring block at 15:30 only if recovered. Sunday uses three anchors: Fajr without feed browsing, one-protein prep at 09:30 for no more than 30 minutes, and a 15-minute review immediately after Dhuhr. If an anchor is missed, resume at the next prayer; do not write off the weekend.

4. **Reels on the main account — Verdict: high-risk; laptop posting alone is insufficient.** The main account removes the shield supplied by account separation. Daily capture can stay, but publishing needs a dedicated browser profile, feed/Explore/Reels blocks, prepared caption, timer, logout, and preferably scheduling. Two browser binges in seven days trigger removal of direct main-account access; no debate in the moment.

5. **Meal prep — Verdict: weekly batch cooking should be abandoned.** The minimum viable protocol is a recurring fixed grocery order, three portions of one protein on Sunday, a nightly two-minute shaker/dates check, and one compliant emergency meal. This fits an ADHD solo lifter better than an all-week cook and creates less waste/cost under ₹12 lakh of debt.

6. **Trackers and auto-pause — Verdict: sound idea, over-engineered implementation; auto-pause is the wrong response.** One SOT is good. The ledger and specialist trackers can supply evidence, but the grid, habit tracker, app streak, and completion-locked day duplicate state. Seven misses should trigger a review, not inactivity. Replace pause with automatic Recovery Mode at the first chain break; never start another numbered arc.

7. **Office free time — Verdict: the existing protocol will remain unused until access and task choice are removed.** Physically store the phone at 12:30, preselect one task before the break, keep the printed sequence card visible, and require only 25 minutes after Dhuhr/food. The other break time may be lunch, walking, or rest. Trying to optimize all two hours makes the phone the easier option.

8. **DSA restart — Verdict: the content choice and anti-Copilot rule are sound; resetting identity/totals and adding two daily sessions are not.** Treat Contains Duplicate, Two Sum, and Valid Anagram as a three-day diagnostic, one problem per office session. Give an unaided attempt 15 minutes, write the approach, take one limited hint if stuck, then try for 10 more minutes. If a solution is viewed, close it and re-code from memory the next day before marking it solved. After the diagnostic, target about three genuinely new problems per week plus review; preserve all historical attempts and label assisted versus unaided.

9. **Restart fatigue — Verdict: V4 currently has no meaningful structural difference from V3.** It has a denser perfect-day blueprint and additional promises, while the known failure path remains intact. Renaming 15 September “Day 1” will likely buy novelty for several days and then repeat the same collapse. The necessary difference is a non-pausing Recovery Mode that activates before one bad night becomes nine missed days.

## WHAT TO KEEP

- **Fajr alarm → prayer → Muay Thai camp.** This held every day and is the strongest existing anchor. Do not burden it with mandatory tahajjud or a difficult new DSA problem.
- **Office salah access and reminders.** Dhuhr and Asr at the office musallah worked better than home prayers. Keep the reminders, Jumu'ah routine, and next-prayer resumption rule.
- **The physical shield direction.** Charger out of the bedroom, 21:00 cutoff, lockbox, blockers, and greyscale target the correct vector. Promote physical distance over greyscale or willpower.
- **The immediate post-training shake and simplified protein list.** A fixed recovery action reduces choice. Simplify preparation; do not redesign the locked diet inside this productivity audit.
- **Daily family contact.** A 15–20 minute post-Isha call is bounded, meaningful, and especially important because nobody locally shares this career-switch journey.
- **Film-and-drop guardrails.** Ten-second clips and no Instagram app separate documenting life from consuming a feed.
- **Anti-Copilot learning.** Unaided attempts and explicit assisted/unaided labels are the right response to the three carryover problems.
- **One source of truth, honest miss detection, and Telegram reminders.** The system noticed the nine misses and paused rather than fabricating progress. Keep the truth; change the response to it. Keep only reminders that lead to one concrete action, not more status administration.
- **TCS containment.** Do the paid job reliably and use only genuinely free, policy-compliant time for career work. With ₹12 lakh debt, jeopardizing current income for an idealized switch routine would be reckless.

## ONE STRUCTURAL CHANGE THAT MATTERS MOST

### Replace `paused/new arc` with automatic **Recovery Mode**

This is the one change that would make V4 genuinely different from V3. Recovery Mode starts the morning after **any one** of these events: phone use after cutoff that delays sleep, re-sleep after Fajr, a post-camp phone binge, or a missed scheduled day caused by binging. It runs for the next three calendar days inside the same continuous history—no reset, no new version, no catch-up.

For those three days, the system shows only one short card:

1. Resume every salah at the next prayer; use office Dhuhr/Asr and the post-Isha family-call anchor.
2. Follow Fajr → camp and, on return, lockbox → shake → shower/dress → office. No bed/sofa.
3. At work, do one preselected 10–25 minute career task with the phone physically away.
4. Capture reel clips if desired, but use only queued/scheduled publishing; no Instagram browsing.
5. No tahajjud target, extra MMA, catch-up work, new tracker setup, or “restart tomorrow.”
6. Phone away after the family call; setup for 10 minutes; sleep target 21:30.

After three calendar days, normal mode resumes automatically. A miss during Recovery Mode starts another three-day Recovery Mode but does not erase anything. Seven consecutive misses still generate an honest review/alert, but never a blank `paused` state.

The governing question must change from **“Can Ramish execute the whole blueprint today?”** to **“When the known chain starts, does the environment force the next small recovery action?”** V4 as drafted does not. Recovery Mode does.
